## 2026-10-05 — Handoff and model scope

**Sources reviewed:** [recursive_dynamics_pressure_coordinate_handoff.md](recursive_dynamics_pressure_coordinate_handoff.md), the local pressure-space `*_nume.m` exports, the historical length-coordinate project branches (`Ozi` and `compare-pointmass-standard`), and the clean `standard-arm-floating-base` implementation.

**Decisions from the researcher:**

- Use pressure itself as the generalized coordinate. The local symbolic transform, Jacobians, Hessians, and section integrals have already been differentiated with respect to pressure. No length-to-pressure coordinate transformation is required.
- Use two independent pressures per section, as in the two-length-change model. For three sections, the intended generalized coordinate ordering is `q = [p12; p13; p22; p23; p32; p33]` in Pa. A local generated function receives `[0, p2, p3]` as a `1x3` row; the leading zero is a reference slot, not a third independent coordinate.
- In each section's local `L = [L(1); L(2); L(3)]` (m), `L(2)` is the flexible section length. `L(1)` and `L(3)` are sensor offsets. They are not lengths of other sections and do not carry mass.
- Sensor geometry may use the full local `L`. For distributed backbone mass integrals, use `Lbody = [0; L(2); 0]` so sensor offsets do not contribute mass. The placement of sensor frames in the future three-section animation and measurement model still needs explicit validation.

**Implementation direction:** adapt the clean reference recursion for pressure-local geometry, then validate local derivatives and integrals, recursive mass and gravity terms, velocity terms, interpreted simulation, animation, and finally MEX parity. The historical length-coordinate functions are references for algorithm structure and validation methods, not substitutes for the pressure exports.

**Open modeling decisions:** the pressure-coordinate generalized input/work law, elastic and damping laws, section-specific physical parameters, gravity convention, and any sensor measurement frames have not yet been established for the three-section model. No three-section pressure dynamics are claimed as validated.

A 24-node Gauss–Legendre rule integrates local point position, Jacobian, and Hessian over `xi ∈ [0,1]` and compares the results with the exported `mu`, `S`, `F`, `E` moments and their pressure derivatives. ## 2026-10-05 — Step 3: interpreted three-section recursive core

**Implementation:** Added [armS_pressure_core.m](armS_pressure_core.m), returning `M` (6×6), `C` (6×6), `G` (6×1), and `dM` (6×6×6) for `q=[p12;p13;p22;p23;p32;p33]` (Pa) and `dq` (Pa/s). Each column of `params.L` is one section's `[base sensor offset; flexible section length; tip sensor offset]` in meters; the validation uses three unequal section lengths. Section mass is uniform in normalized material coordinate, `dm_i=m_i dxi`. Only translational kinetic energy is modeled. Each section's mass moments and the tip transform used for propagation receive `Lbody=[0;params.L(2,i);0]`, so sensor offsets affect neither the mass terms nor the physical connection between section backbones. The local pressure exports are used directly; no length-coordinate transformation is applied.

**Dynamics convention:** `params.g` is gravitational acceleration in the model frame. The potential is `V(q)=-Σ_i m_i∫gᵀx_i(q,xi) dxi`, and `G=∂V/∂q`. The intended equation is `M(q) qdd + C(q,dq) dq + G(q) = Q`, with the pressure-coordinate generalized input `Q` still to be derived. `C` is assembled from `dM` with the Christoffel convention used in the handoff. The current local-local mass block treats the polynomial `R` as orthogonal; the approximation is measured below.

**Independent validation:** Added [validate_armS_pressure_core.m](validate_armS_pressure_core.m). The reference composes section transforms directly, estimates global point Jacobians by centered 2 Pa pressure differences, and integrates `JᵀJ` and `-Jᵀg` with 20-node Gauss–Legendre quadrature. It checks `dM` with centered 20 Pa differences of `M`, `Mdot-2C` skew symmetry, mass symmetry and Cholesky positive definiteness, and invariance when only the massless sensor offsets change. At the second pose, it also compares `G` with a finite-difference gradient of the directly integrated potential.

The test parameters were `L(2,:)=[0.278,0.260,0.290]` m, `r=[0.013;0.0125;0.014]` m, `K=[0.0226;0.021;0.024]` (current geometry parameters; units remain to be reviewed), `A=pi*(r/2).^2` m², `m=[0.10;0.08;0.12]` kg, `g=[0;0;-9.81]` m/s², and `dq=[100;-70;50;20;-40;80]` Pa/s. ## 2026-10-05 — Step 4: passive pressure-coordinate dynamics

## 2026-10-05 — Interpretation of pressure coordinates and physical actuation

**Research discussion:** A passively falling, vented arm can change shape while its chamber gauge pressures stay approximately zero. Therefore the six simulated coordinates currently called pressures must not be interpreted automatically as measured chamber pressures. They are pressure-valued parameters of the chosen shape map (an equivalent-pressure shape chart). A nonzero value during the passive simulation labels a deformed shape; it does not claim the vented chamber has acquired that pressure. A sealed chamber is different: deformation can change its volume and pressure even with no valve command. Whether the current pressure-to-geometry map remains valid under gravity and external loading must be checked, especially if it was originally fitted or derived from unloaded quasi-static actuation equilibria.

**Meaning of the current input:** Since `q` is measured in Pa, its generalized force satisfies `δW=Q_qᵀδq` and has units `J/Pa=m³`. This is a virtual-work conjugate quantity, not a physical pressure command. If a mechanical shape vector `s` obeys `s=f(q)` with Jacobian `J_f=∂s/∂q`, then a mechanical generalized force `F_s` maps to `Q_q=J_fᵀF_s`. For chamber gauge pressures `p_ch` and volume map `V(s)`, pneumatic virtual work is `δW=p_chᵀδV`, yielding `Q_q=(∂V/∂q)ᵀp_ch=J_fᵀ(∂V/∂s)ᵀp_ch` under the stated holonomic map. These equations are a proposed physical input derivation; chamber volume functions and the validity of the shape map have not been established for this arm.

**Modeling consequence:** For a physically interpretable passive/actuated comparison, keep mechanical shape (length or curvature, or a clearly labeled pressure-equivalent shape coordinate) distinct from actual chamber-pressure states. Vented passive motion uses approximately zero chamber gauge pressure but may have nonzero shape coordinates. A pneumatic model would add chamber volumes, mass-flow/valve dynamics, pressure states, and a virtual-work coupling. The present `inputForce` remains an abstract generalized force until that derivation and calibration are done. The Figure 2 title and runner comments were updated to label the plotted trajectories as pressure-equivalent shape coordinates; they are not predicted sensor readings.

**Research context:** This separation of mechanical coordinates and pneumatic pressure states, and the pressure-volume work coupling, is consistent with [Stölzle and Della Santina, *Piston-Driven Pneumatically-Actuated Soft Robots: Modeling and Backstepping Control*](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/103544037/Piston_Driven_Pneumatically_Actuated_Soft_Robots_Modeling_and_Backstepping_Control.pdf) and [*Nonlinear energy-based control of soft continuum pneumatic manipulators*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11071-021-06817-1). Their constitutive and pneumatic assumptions are not adopted automatically here.

## 2026-10-05 — Potential control use of the pressure-equivalent model

**Research discussion:** The recursive `M,C,G` and direct pressure-coordinate geometry may be useful for trajectory prediction, inverse kinematics, gravity and motion feedforward, and an outer shape/tip controller if the six pressure-equivalent coordinates form a reliable, invertible chart of the measured arm shape over the operating range. The same coordinates could be estimated from shape sensors; their values must not be substituted with chamber-pressure gauge readings during transient or externally loaded motion. The present model is not yet a validated pressure-command-to-motion plant.

**Proposed control architecture, not implemented:** A desired shape or tip trajectory could be represented by `q_eq*`; the mechanical model plus feedback could request a generalized effort `Q_eq*`. A separately derived actuation map from chamber volume or measured actuator force would allocate that effort to physically feasible chamber pressures, and a pneumatic/valve inner loop would track those pressures. If three physical chambers per section are used with only two independent shape coordinates, allocation must account for the extra actuation direction, nonnegative gauge pressures, and hardware limits. The elastic energy and pneumatic work must be defined consistently to avoid counting the same actuation twice.

**Evidence needed before dynamic control claims:** Verify the shape chart against independent shape measurements under gravity, external loads, and transient pressure changes; derive or identify the actuator generalized-force map and pneumatic dynamics; then compare predicted motion and control performance with experiments. The current passive trajectory and energy checks establish internal numerical consistency only.

## 2026-10-13 — PDI feedback linearization research discussion

**Feasible mathematical controller:** Interpreting PDI as proportional-derivative feedback with integral action, the current six-coordinate model supports an *ideal generalized-effort* computed-torque controller. Write `h(q,dq)=C(q,dq)dq+G(q)+F_el(q)+D dq`, `e=q_d-q`, `z_dot=e`, `v=qdd_d+K_D e_dot+K_P e+K_I z`, and `Q*=M(q)v+h(q,dq)`. If `M` is nonsingular, `q,dq` are available, the model is exact, and all six components of `Q*` can be applied instantaneously, then `e_dd+K_D e_dot+K_P e+K_I z=0`. For independent diagonal axes, positive gains and `K_D K_P>K_I` componentwise satisfy the cubic Routh-Hurwitz condition; arbitrary positive matrix gains alone do not prove stability. The gains should be designed in normalized coordinates because `q` is measured in Pa. This controller can be implemented and compared in simulation with the symbolic or compiled RHS without claiming hardware actuation.

**Physical input gap:** `q` is a pressure-equivalent *shape* coordinate, which need not equal any measured chamber pressure during passive motion. The current `Q` has units `J/Pa=m^3`; it is neither chamber pressure nor a valve command. For nine actual chambers, let `p_ch∈R^9` be gauge pressures and `V(q)∈R^9` a measured or derived chamber-volume map. Under the specific virtual-work assumption `δW=p_ch^TδV`, the candidate mechanical effort map is `Q=B(q)p_ch`, `B(q)=(∂V/∂q)^T∈R^(6×9)`. This map is not yet available or validated. A pressure allocator would need `rank(B)=6` in the controlled region and a feasible solution under pressure, flow, and common-mode constraints; otherwise exact six-coordinate feedback linearization is unavailable. It must also avoid counting pneumatic work twice alongside the current elastic potential. A valve command acts through chamber pressure dynamics, such as mass flow and changing volume, so the computed `Q*` is at most a desired *outer-loop* effort until a pressure/valve inner loop or full pneumatic model is added. Exact valve-level feedback linearization cannot be claimed from the current mechanical RHS or MEX timing.

**First actuation-map hypothesis to test:** The existing gauge relation `Δell=(3 A r²/(2K))[-q_2-q_3;q_2;q_3]` for one section gives, under an additional constant effective volume-area assumption `ΔV=A_V Δell`, the illustrative map `Q_section=A_V(3 A r²/(2K))*[p_ch,2-p_ch,1; p_ch,3-p_ch,1]`. The two independent pressure differences could then span the two section efforts, while a common pressure component lies in this simplified map's nullspace. This is a derivation from assumed actuator volume geometry, not a calibrated result. PMA radius/volume changes, sign conventions, pressure-dependent stiffness, and the unresolved units of `K` can invalidate it; measure `V(q)` or generalized force before using it in a controller or paper claim.

**RoboSoft paper direction:** A defensible contribution would connect the three-section recursive model and real-time MEX to a calibrated nine-chamber-to-six-effort map, constrained pressure allocation, sensing/estimation of shape and velocity, and a pressure-controlled or valve-controlled implementation. Compare tracking and disturbance rejection against transparent PD/PID and simpler model-based baselines, across trajectories, speeds, loads, and pressure limits; report shape/tip error, chamber-pressure tracking, latency, and model mismatch. A simulation of ideal `Q*` alone would demonstrate the mathematical controller but not pneumatic feedback linearization. The MEX is a numerical approximation to the symbolic model with the high-bend parity limit documented above; validate the controller using both variants and under parameter uncertainty. Feedback linearization with integral action has already been reported for a single bending pneumatic actuator, and task-space feedback linearization has been reported for continuum robots, so novelty should rest on the validated three-section model-to-actuator connection rather than the control law alone.

**Relevant prior work:** [Nonlinear Estimation and Control of Bending Soft Pneumatic Actuators Using Feedback Linearization and UKF](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9735162) reports a mechanical-plus-pressure model, observer, feedback linearization, and integral action for one actuator. [Task-Space Control of Continuum Robots using Underactuated Discrete Rod Models](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9982271/) reports input-output feedback linearization for an underactuated continuum model. [Design and Control of Pneumatic Systems for Soft Robotics: A Simulation Approach](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9447215/) treats pressure-loop and pneumatic hardware design. [Nonlinear energy-based control of soft continuum pneumatic manipulators](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11071-021-06817-1) explicitly models chamber pressure dynamics and flow input. These references inform the proposed path; their assumptions are not automatically adopted by this arm.

## 2026-10-13 — Section-angle to pressure-equivalent reference trajectories

**Angle convention and inverse:** In [pressure2config.m](pressure2config.m), `theta=c(1)` is the bending-plane direction `atan2[-sqrt(3)(p2-p3),3(p2+p3)]`, while `phi=c(2)` is the nonnegative bend magnitude before that helper wraps it to `[-pi,pi]`. This differs from the frequently used convention where theta is bend magnitude. Algebraic inversion gives, for each section with `s=K/(A*r)`, `q2=s*phi*(cos(theta)/3-sin(theta)/sqrt(3))` and `q3=s*phi*(cos(theta)/3+sin(theta)/sqrt(3))`. Equivalently use smooth bend components `u=phi*cos(theta)`, `v=phi*sin(theta)` and `q2=s*(u/3-v/sqrt(3))`, `q3=s*(u/3+v/sqrt(3))`. This formulation maps a straight section to zero coordinates without requiring a defined bending-plane angle there. Three independently specified section pairs generate the six-coordinate reference `q_ref`; one global tip-angle pair is insufficient and requires multisection inverse kinematics or another redundancy-selection rule.

**Implementation:** Added [config2pressure_reference_N3.m](config2pressure_reference_N3.m), accepting `3xN` arrays of section `theta`, `phi` and the existing `r,K,A` parameters, returning interleaved `6xN` `qRef`. If smooth angle rates and accelerations are supplied, it analytically returns `dqRef` and `ddqRef` for the ideal computed-effort controller; no finite differences are used by the function. The supported inverse branch uses `0 <= phi < pi`, since the older helper wraps its second output. Desired bend components or section angles should be planned smoothly; direct angle interpolation through the straight shape can make `theta` discontinuous. The reference entries have Pa units but remain pressure-equivalent *shape coordinates*, not nine actual chamber-pressure setpoints. The unresolved physical units of `K` and experimental shape-map validity still apply.

## 2026-10-13 — Why angle-to-pressure inversion alone may suffice, and when dynamics helps

**Researcher's challenge:** If the inverse `theta/phi` map gives usable chamber-pressure commands, why is a pressure-coordinate dynamic model needed? This is a valid objection. The algebraic inverse prescribes pressures for a geometry according to the current constitutive/kinematic relationship; it contains no desired velocity, acceleration, gravity/load correction, actuator delay, or hysteresis. If an actual pressure regulator applies those setpoints and the arm tracks the desired shape accurately over the intended speeds and loads, a dynamic controller may offer little practical improvement. Direct inverse-map pressure control is therefore the essential experimental baseline, not something to dismiss.

**Dynamic-model value conditional on a physical input map:** A mechanical dynamic model can predict generalized effort needed to produce acceleration and compensate `C*dq`, gravity, elasticity, and damping. In a simple illustrative angle model `I*theta_dd+b*theta_d+k*theta=a*p_ch`, static pressure inversion gives `p_static=k*theta_d/a` at rest, whereas a moving trajectory requires `(I*theta_dd+b*theta_d+k*theta)/a` before feedback and constraints. The present recursive model provides the six-coordinate mechanical left-hand side in a pressure-equivalent shape chart, but its right-hand input `Q` is **not** actual chamber pressure or a valve command. Without an independently validated map from nine chamber pressures to six generalized efforts, it cannot compute the needed physical pressure correction. A regulator can make explicit valve-flow modeling optional for an outer controller if its pressure tracking is measured and sufficiently fast; it does not remove the need for an actuation map.

**Modeling decision for control/paper:** Treat the new [config2pressure_reference_N3.m](config2pressure_reference_N3.m) output as a shape-coordinate reference unless a separate experimental test establishes that its values also equal appropriate chamber-pressure setpoints under the intended conditions. The passive test already shows why identifying the coordinate with physical chamber pressure is inconsistent: a vented arm can deform while measured gauge pressure stays near zero. Two coherent paths remain: (1) use shape coordinates, retain the recursive dynamics, identify `Q=B(q,p_ch)` (or `B(q)p_ch`) and pressure allocation, then add pressure-loop dynamics as needed; or (2) formulate a mechanical-coordinate model with physical chamber pressures as separate states/inputs. Merely renaming `q` as chamber pressure or passing `Q*` to a valve would not close this gap. Compare direct inverse-map pressure tracking with a calibrated model-based controller on the same three-section hardware across speed, payload, and disturbances; only measured improvement justifies a dynamic-control claim. If direct pressure tracking already meets the task requirements, the dynamics may remain useful for simulation, load prediction, or other tasks but is not necessary for that controller.

## 2026-10-14 — Proposed voltage-to-chamber-pressure-to-shape system model

**Question and conclusion:** The researcher proposes treating the 0–10 V valve command as input, pressure as output/shape, and using the pressure-length Jacobian to connect the existing pressure-coordinate dynamics with a complete pneumatic system. This is a feasible *augmented model* if physical chamber pressures are added as separate states and the voltage-driven device is identified. The present six `q` entries are pressure-equivalent mechanical shape coordinates, whereas the arm has nine actual chamber pressures `p_ch`. The mechanical state may be `[q;dq]` (12 states), pneumatic state `p_ch` (9 states), and command `u∈[0,10]^9 V`; further spool, line, or regulator states may be necessary. Chamber pressure is a measured output `y_p=p_ch`, while shape/tip pose is a distinct mechanical output `y_s=h(q)` or `h(ell)`. Pressure alone cannot generally reveal shape under gravity, external load, or passive vented motion.

**What the pressure-length Jacobian does:** Away from the removable straight-shape singularity in [pressure2length.m](pressure2length.m), one section's kinematic relation simplifies to `ell_1=-c(q_2+q_3)`, `ell_2=c q_2`, `ell_3=c q_3`, with `c=3 A r²/(2K)`. Thus `J_ell,q=c*[-1,-1;1,0;0,1]` per section, or `c*I_2` for the independent length pair. This maps **mechanical shape coordinates**, not a time-varying physical pressure state to length by causality. With a calibrated nine-chamber volume model `V(ell)` and gauge pressures, virtual work gives `tau_ell=(∂V/∂ell)^T p_ch` and `Q_q=J_ell,q^T tau_ell=(∂V/∂q)^T p_ch`. The volume derivative, PMA sign convention, effective area, and unresolved `K` units must be measured or derived. A Jacobian alone does not supply the pressure-to-force law or voltage-to-pressure dynamics. If reusing the length-based mechanical model, transform its kinetic/potential/dissipation laws consistently and reconcile the known gravity-sign and stiffness-definition differences before claiming equivalence to the pressure core.

**Candidate coupled equations:** In pressure-equivalent mechanical coordinates, a proposed model is `M(q)qdd+C(q,dq)dq+G(q)+F_el(q)+D dq=B_q(q)p_ch+Q_ext`, with `B_q=(∂V/∂q)^T` only under the specified chamber virtual-work model and with gauge pressures/signs handled consistently. For a raw mass-flow valve, one isothermal ideal-gas candidate for each chamber is `pdot_abs=(R_s*T/V(q))*mdot(u,p_supply,p_abs,p_atm)-(p_abs/V(q))*Vdot(q,dq)`; here `mdot` is signed and requires the actual valve/line characteristics, `Vdot=(∂V/∂q)dq`, and `p_abs=p_gauge+p_atm`. If the 0–10 V device is instead a closed-loop proportional **pressure regulator**, a measured command-to-pressure transfer model (possibly first-order with saturation, rate limits, and deadband) may replace the explicit valve-flow equation over its validated bandwidth. Voltage scaling alone establishes neither model. Avoid double-counting pressure-generated elastic work in `F_el` and `B_q p_ch`.

**Hardware clarification and control sequence:** The researcher confirmed that each 0–10 V signal drives a **pressure regulator**, not a raw proportional flow valve. Thus the first identified input block should be `p_cmd=f_u(u)` plus the regulator/chamber closed-loop response from command voltage to measured chamber pressure. A first-order `p_ch_dot=(p_cmd-p_ch)/tau` model is only a starting hypothesis; identify rise/fall asymmetry, bandwidth, saturation, deadband, line volume, and response under changing chamber volume. An explicit orifice/mass-flow model is optional if the measured regulator response is sufficient for the intended operating range. Measure chamber pressure and independent shape/length during static and dynamic tests; identify `V(ell)` or actuator force/effort versus pressure and shape, then validate rank and feasible allocation of nine pressures to six mechanical efforts. Compare direct inverse-kinematic pressure commands against a coupled model-based controller on trajectories, speeds, and loads. A control cascade would run `desired shape → mechanical effort Q* → feasible chamber-pressure targets → regulator voltage commands`, with measured pressure and shape feedback. Only after experimental validation should this be described as a complete voltage-to-shape system model. The regulator's internal valve dynamics need not be claimed or modeled unless its externally observed behavior requires them.

**Related primary studies:** [Nonlinear Estimation and Control of Bending Soft Pneumatic Actuators Using Feedback Linearization and UKF](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9735162) models both motion and pressure dynamics for a valve-driven actuator. [Design and Control of Pneumatic Systems for Soft Robotics: A Simulation Approach](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9447215/) examines valve conductance, receiver pressure, and closed-loop pressure response. These are precedents for model structure, not evidence that their parameters or assumptions apply to this arm.
